**Looking for Fruit Not Flash in a Church**  
================================================================

Introduction  
When we walk into a church, it’s easy to be drawn to the bright lights, polished programs, or charismatic speakers. Yet what sustains a community over time is not the flash but the fruit—love, joy, peace, patience, kindness, goodness, faithfulness, gentleness, and self‑control that grow from a life rooted in Christ. This article invites us to look for those quiet, lasting signs of spiritual health as we seek a place to belong and serve.

## Scripture Foundation  

> **Scripture Reflection:** The Bible repeatedly points us to fruit as the true evidence of a life with God.  

- **Galatians 5:22‑23** – “But the fruit of the Spirit is love, joy, peace, forbearance, kindness, goodness, faithfulness, gentleness and self‑control. Against such things there is no law.” *(Scripture quotations are from the New International Version (NIV))*  
  This passage tells us what genuine spiritual life looks like: qualities that build up rather than impress.

- **Matthew 7:15‑20** – “Watch out for false prophets… By their fruit you will recognize them.” *(NIV)*  
  Jesus warns that outward show can be deceiving; the real test is what a community produces in character and action.

- **James 3:17‑18** – “But the wisdom that comes from heaven is first of all pure; then peace‑loving, considerate, submissive, full of mercy and good fruit, impartial and sincere. Peacemakers who sow in peace reap a harvest of righteousness.” *(NIV)*  
  Heavenly wisdom yields gentle, merciful fruit—exactly the tone we hope to find in a church family.

- **John 15:5** – “I am the vine; you are the branches. If you remain in me and I in you, you will bear much fruit; apart from me you can do nothing.” *(NIV)*  
  Fruit flows from staying connected to Christ, not from polished performances.

Together, these passages guide us to evaluate a church not by its spectacle but by the Spirit‑produced love, peace, and service evident in its people.

## What This Looks Like in Daily Life  

- **Listening more than performing:** In a healthy congregation, people ask, “How are you really?” and wait for an answer, rather than rushing to share a testimony or announce the next event.  
- **Serving without spotlight:** You’ll see members quietly setting up chairs, bringing meals to a grieving family, or tutoring a child—acts done because they love, not because they’ll be thanked publicly.  
- **Humility in leadership:** Leaders admit mistakes, seek counsel, and prioritize the flock’s growth over personal platform.  
- **Grace-filled conflict resolution:** When disagreements arise, the community pursues reconciliation, listening, and forgiveness, reflecting the peace of Christ.  
- **Joy that persists:** Even in hard seasons, there’s a steady undercurrent of gratitude and hope that points to reliance on God rather than circumstances.  

These signs are the fruit Jesus described—observable, repeatable, and rooted in the Spirit’s work.

## Questions for Reflection  

1. When you think of a church you’ve attended, what specific actions or attitudes made you feel genuinely cared for?  
2. In what ways might you be unintentionally valuing “flash” (e.g., impressive music, large crowds) over the quieter fruit of the Spirit?  
3. How can you contribute to a culture of love, patience, and kindness in your current church or small group?  
4. Recall a time when you saw a leader admit a fault. How did that affect your trust and willingness to engage?  
5. What practical steps can you take to discern whether a community’s fruit is healthy before committing long‑term?  
6. How does remaining “in the vine” (John 15:5) shape your expectations for what a church should produce?  

## A Simple Practice This Week  

> **Practice:** Spend 10 minutes each day reading Galatians 5:22‑23 and asking the Holy Spirit to highlight one fruit you’d like to grow in your interactions with others.  
> **Next Step:** Choose one concrete way to exhibit that fruit today—perhaps offering a listening ear to a coworker, extending patience to a family member, or performing a small act of service without expecting recognition.

## Key Takeaways  

- True spiritual health is measured by the fruit of the Spirit, not by flashy programs.  
- Jesus teaches that we recognize genuine faith by what a community produces.  
- Practical signs of fruit include humble service, attentive listening, grace‑filled conflict, and steady joy.  
- Regularly reflecting on Scripture helps us align our expectations with God’s design.  
- Small, intentional actions—like practicing one fruit each day—help us cultivate and recognize healthy church life.
