**Following Jesus in Your Workplace**  
==================================================

Introduction  
Many of us spend the majority of our waking hours at work, and it can be easy to feel that our faith belongs only to Sunday mornings or small‑group meetings. Yet Jesus taught that our everyday labor is a place where we can love God and love our neighbors in tangible ways. This article offers a gentle, Scripture‑grounded look at how following Jesus shapes our attitudes, actions, and relationships on the job.

## Scripture Foundation  
> **Scripture Reflection:** Jesus calls us to be “salt and light” in the world (Matthew 5:13‑16, NIV). He also reminds us that whatever we do, we should work “as working for the Lord, not for human masters” (Colossians 3:23‑24, NIV).  
> Scripture quotations are from the New International Version (NIV).  

These passages show that our work is not separate from our worship. When we act with integrity, serve others, and seek excellence, we reflect God’s character. The prophet Micah summarizes the heart of this calling: “He has shown you, O mortal, what is good. And what does the Lord require of you? To act justly and to love mercy and to walk humbly with your God” (Micah 6:8, NIV). The apostle James adds that faith without deeds is dead (James 2:14‑17, NIV), reminding us that our beliefs should shape how we treat coworkers, customers, and even competitors.

## What This Looks Like in Daily Life  
- **Speaking truth with kindness:** When a project runs behind schedule, we can offer honest feedback while also acknowledging the effort others have put in.  
- **Serving beyond the job description:** Helping a teammate learn a new tool, covering a shift for someone dealing with a family issue, or simply listening when a colleague is stressed.  
- **Practicing fairness:** Ensuring that credit is shared, advocating for equitable pay or opportunities, and refusing to participate in gossip that harms reputations.  
- **Maintaining humility:** Admitting mistakes quickly, asking for help when needed, and recognizing that our success is a gift from God rather than solely our own achievement.  
- **Seeking peace:** If conflict arises, we pursue reconciliation rather than victory, following Jesus’ call to be peacemakers (Matthew 5:9).

## Questions for Reflection  
1. In what ways do you currently see your work as an opportunity to love God and love your neighbor?  
2. Where do you feel tension between workplace expectations and your Christian convictions?  
3. How might practicing “salt and light” change the atmosphere of your team or department?  
4. What small act of justice or mercy could you introduce this week without overstepping your role?  
5. How does remembering that you work “for the Lord” affect your attitude toward routine or tedious tasks?  
6. Who is one person at work you could pray for or encourage in a specific way this coming week?

## A Simple Practice This Week  
> **Practice:** Choose one concrete action that reflects Micah 6:8—act justly, love mercy, walk humbly—and carry it out intentionally during your workday. For example, you might compliment a coworker’s effort publicly, volunteer to take on a task no one else wants, or pause to listen without interrupting when someone shares a concern.  
> After you’ve done it, take a brief moment to thank God for the opportunity to serve and ask for wisdom to continue growing in faithfulness at work.

## Prayer  
Gracious God, thank you for the gift of work and the ways it allows us to reflect your love. Help us to see each task, each conversation, and each challenge as a chance to honor you and serve others. Give us courage to act justly, compassion to show mercy, and humility to walk closely with you throughout our days. May our workplaces become places where your light shines brightly. In Jesus’ name we pray, Amen.

## Key Takeaways  
- Our work is a field for living out the greatest commandments: love God and love neighbor.  
- Scripture calls us to be salt and light, to work as for the Lord, and to practice justice, mercy, and humility.  
- Small, intentional actions—kind words, helpful deeds, fair attitudes—can transform our workplace culture.  
- Regular reflection and prayer keep our faith integrated with our daily labor.  
- Growth happens gradually; we invite God’s guidance one step at a time.
